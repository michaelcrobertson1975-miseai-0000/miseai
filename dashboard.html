        Food Orders

        How MiseAI sees the order

        The chef stays in control of purchasing. MiseAI keeps the count, par, vendor pack, saved order record, and later invoice reconciliation tied together so the system can verify what was intended against what was actually billed.

        1 · Count
        Chef enters On Hand.

        This is the physical kitchen count. It updates the ingredient record and gives MiseAI the starting point for the order.

        2 · Par
        Par stays manual and restaurant-controlled.

        The chef can change Par when the business changes. Postgres calculates Need as the shortage between Par and On Hand.

        3 · Pack & billing
        Need and Order Qty are not the same thing.

        Need stays in the kitchen count unit. Order Qty stays in the vendor purchase unit. A 16 lb shortage may require 1 CS or 2 CS depending on the known pack. Catch-weight products keep estimated weight separate from final billed weight.

        4 · Chef order
        The chef enters what was actually ordered.

        MiseAI does not place the vendor order. After the chef orders from the company, Record Order saves that vendor batch, item/SKU, quantity, unit, expected price, pack information, and catch-weight status.

        5 · Invoice arrives
        The existing invoice system structures the document.

        The PDF or photo is read into invoice rows. The original invoice remains the source document; Postgres works from the structured fields after parsing.

        6 · Postgres reconciliation
        Deterministic checks compare order intent with the invoice.

        Vendor/SKU or ingredient identity is matched first, then quantity and unit/pack behavior, then price and math. Catch-weight lines use their own weight-aware rules instead of fixed-case math.

        Verification path
        Three-part verification before a clean match is trusted.

        The system checks the chef's saved order, the parsed invoice line, and deterministic Postgres unit/price math before a clean match is shown. A disagreement is surfaced for review instead of being silently corrected or guessed.

        Order intentInvoice linePostgres math
        Why use it
        The chef keeps the decision; MiseAI keeps the audit trail.

        That gives the restaurant a reliable record of what was counted, what was needed, what was actually ordered, and what the vendor later billed. Price changes, quantity differences, unit mismatches, catch-weight exceptions, and unrecognized lines can be surfaced to the existing Review Queue without taking purchasing judgment away from the chef.

        Beverage Orders

        How MiseAI sees beverage ordering

        The full beverage catalog stays available. Vendor tabs answer who you buy from; category tabs answer what you are counting or ordering.

        1 · Inventory
        On Hand comes from the real beverage inventory.

        The order list does not disappear when an item is at par. Every active SKU remains available as the restaurant's permanent buying catalog.

        2 · Vendor + category
        Use either direction.

        Open a vendor and work that order, or open a beverage category and see that category across multiple vendors.

        3 · SKU identity
        Vendor name and vendor SKU stay together.

        The same bottle can have different item numbers at different distributors. MiseAI keeps the vendor-specific SKU with the purchase record so later invoice matching does not guess.

        4 · Purchase unit
        Cases and eaches are both valid.

        Beer and wine may be bought by CS; premium bottles may be EA; draft beer may be KEG or 1/2 BBL. Need stays separate from the vendor purchase unit.

        5 · Record the order
        The chef or bar manager records what was actually ordered.

        MiseAI saves one vendor batch at a time with SKU, quantity, purchase unit, pack and expected price. It does not send the order to the distributor.

        6 · Invoice check
        The existing invoice system verifies what arrived.

        The saved vendor order can be reconciled against the parsed invoice by vendor/SKU or linked inventory identity, then quantity, unit and price.

        Why use it
        The whole cellar stays visible while each vendor order stays clean.

        A restaurant can carry hundreds of beverage SKUs without scrolling one giant list. Vendor and category tabs shorten the working view without deleting or hiding the permanent catalog.
